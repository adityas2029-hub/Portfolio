Quiet wins in food service ops.

An internal tool that trimmed 22% off prep-to-plate. A masterclass in designing for people who really don’t want to be designed for.

COVER · drop in kitchen tablet shot
§ 01

Context

A 40-outlet restaurant group was leaking time between order and plate. Not because the kitchen was slow — because the tools made the kitchen do the same thing six times.

The brief asked for a “better dashboard”. The kitchen asked for nothing at all — which is, in operations design, the loudest thing anyone can say.

“I don’t look at the screen. I cook. The screen tells the next person what I cooked.”
§ 02

The problem

Reduce time-to-plate without adding a single new step for kitchen staff. Every change had to feel like less.

  • Five legacy systems, all owned by different parts of the org.
  • Greasy fingers, wet hands, 6-second average glance at the screen.
  • Floor managers wanted more data; line cooks wanted less. Both were right.
§ 03

Process

Two weeks shadowing service across 5 outlets. Then we redrew the data flow so the system did the work the staff used to. UI followed.

A design system for high-glance UI — type sizing, status colour, sound — that we field-tested under real conditions, not in a meeting room.

§ 05

Reflections

The most rewarding feedback was the absence of feedback. Two weeks after rollout, the head chef walked past me without comment. That was the win. The lesson: in ops design, when nobody mentions the tool, you’ve done your job.
